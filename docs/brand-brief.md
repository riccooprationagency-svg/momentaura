## 2. Brand foundation

### The name

[Certain] the current header reads "Moment aura.store" — a space in the middle and the top-level domain inside the wordmark.

[Certain] the namespace is crowded: Momentara, Momentora, shopmoment.com, The Moments Shop, Meilleur Moment all exist and rank. [Likely] `.store` reads as low-trust to shoppers because dropshipping operations use it heavily.

[Certain] you have no traffic, no rankings, no repeat customers and no brand recognition. Switching cost is as low as it will ever be. The domain cost under KSh 2,000; do not let that sunk cost decide a name you keep for a decade.

**Three directions:**

1. **Keep and tighten** — `MOMENTAURA` as one word, all caps, TLD removed from the wordmark. Cheapest. Still says nothing about what you sell.
2. **Say what you do** — built on *keepsake, mark, imprint, crest, token*. E.g. Imprint & Co., Crest Gifting. Credible to a procurement buyer, searchable, descriptive.
3. **Own local ground** — a Swahili or Nairobi-rooted name signals local, reachable, accountable. The opposite of the anonymous-dropship read.

**Recommendation:** direction 2. Corporate buyers search functionally, not poetically.

### Positioning line

> Branded gift sets for Nairobi companies — designed, packed and delivered in five days.

Specific, checkable, and it answers the two questions a B2B buyer actually has. Every element is falsifiable, which is the point.

### Target buyer

[Guessing] until validated: an office manager, HR lead, or marketing coordinator at a Nairobi SME, agency, bank, NGO, sacco or church, ordering 20–100 units for end-of-year staff gifts, client thank-yous, or new-hire onboarding kits. Decides by phone and WhatsApp. Pays by invoice, bank transfer or paybill — **not** by card at a self-serve checkout.

That last point drives the entire architecture. See §7 and §12.

### North star

**Plain. Exact. Dependable.**

Test every decision against those three words. "Aura" fails all three — which is the strongest argument for renaming.

## 3. Voice and copy rules

### The core principle

You are not competing with Rolex. You are competing with the Instagram vendor who takes the M-Pesa and goes quiet.

That is a much lower bar and a completely different brief. Your buyer's question is not *"is this aspirational?"* — it is *"will a real person actually send me this thing?"*

**Trust is the aesthetic.** Every luxury signal you cannot back up costs you, because a buyer registers the cheapest signal in the room, not the most expensive one.

### Banned words

Never use: *elevate, aura, moments, curated, signature, timeless, luxury, premium, bespoke, experience, journey.*

[Certain] "SIGNATURE COLLECTION" and "THE APEX OF CUSTOM HOROLOGY" are borrowed prestige language with no prestige behind them. They widen the gap between what you promise and what the photograph shows, and that gap is where trust dies.

### Rules

- **Concrete nouns and numbers.** "Steel flask, A5 leather notebook, ballpoint. Boxed. KSh 4,400 per set, minimum 10."
- **Sentence case everywhere.** `THE CRUSH BUCKET HAT` shouts. "Crush bucket hat" states. Caps are what brands use when they have nothing specific to say.
- **Product names describe contents.** `Daily set — flask, notebook, pen`, not `THE DAILY SET — CORPORATE GIFTING COLLECTION`. Say what is in the box.
- **Specifics where adjectives currently sit.** "Boxed, delivered in Nairobi within 5 days" outperforms "curated" for trust, every time.
- **No exclamation marks. No "please". No "simply" or "just".**
- **Prices always visible.** Never "contact for price" on catalogue items under KSh 10,000.

## 4. Visual identity

### Direction

Light, not dark.

[Likely] dark backgrounds flatter expensive photography and punish cheap photography — there is no light to reveal texture, so mediocre product shots read as murky rather than moody. Switching to warm off-white will improve your existing photographs without reshooting anything.

[Certain] your current product names render as grey on near-black at the edge of readable. Text a customer must squint at signals that you either did not notice or did not care. Both are disqualifying for someone deciding whether to send you money.

### Palette

| Role | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Paper | `#F7F4EF` | Page background |
| Ink | `#1C1B19` | Headings, body, prices |
| Muted | `#6E6A62` | Secondary text, descriptions |
| Accent | `#1D9E75` | Buttons, delivery info — two uses per page maximum |
| Rule | `#E4DFD5` | Hairlines, dividers, card borders |

[Guessing] worth testing: deep green sits adjacent to the M-Pesa colour world in Kenya, so it may carry a faint "payment confirmed" association. Use it sparingly on transactional elements — delivery promises, add-to-cart, submit — and nowhere decorative.

### Typography

- One workhorse sans for everything: **Inter** or **Instrument Sans** (both free).
- Optionally one serif reserved *only* for page headings.
- **Two weights: 400 regular and 500 medium.** Nothing heavier. Heavy weights are what thin brands use to feel substantial.
- Body 16px minimum, line-height 1.6–1.7. Product names 15px/500.

### Layout

- **Space, because it is free.** Cheap sites are crowded; confident sites are empty. Whitespace is the one premium signal that costs nothing and cannot be read as overclaiming.
- Maximum content width ~1200px. Generous margins.
- Product grid: 3 across on desktop, 2 on tablet, 1 on mobile.
- Prices and key detail visible on the card — no click required.

### Motion

Almost none. No fades, no parallax, no reveal-on-scroll, no hover zoom.

Restraint reads as competence. Effects read as someone hiding thin content behind movement. The two exceptions are in §7.

### If you go anime instead

Invert nearly all of the above. Dark is correct, saturated colour is correct, energy and noise are the point, because you are selling to people who want the work to feel loud. The two directions are genuinely incompatible — which is the fork problem expressed visually.

## 5. Photography standard

**If you do only one thing on this list, do this one.**

[Likely] consistency in photography will do more for how expensive your store feels than colour, type and layout combined — because inconsistency is the specific tell that a store is assembled from other people's images rather than stocked with real things.

[Certain] your current grid mixes: white-background mockups, a phone snap on paving stones, AI-generated street models, and soft-focus stock lifestyle. Four visual languages in one grid.

### The rule

Every product photographed on **one surface, in one light, at one angle.**

- **Surface:** a paper-white or warm-grey sweep. A large sheet of cartridge paper works.
- **Light:** indirect daylight from a window, side-on. Midday. No flash, no lamps.
- **Angle:** pick one — straight-on or 30° — and never deviate.
- **Kit:** your phone, a KSh 500 tripod. That is genuinely sufficient.
- **Post:** crop to the same aspect ratio, correct white balance identically across the set. Nothing else.

### Rules for what must go

- No supplier photographs.
- No AI-generated models.
- No blank mockups presented as products. [Certain] "THE SHINOBI HOODIE" is a photograph of a plain white hoodie with nothing printed on it, and "Crew Neck T-Shirt" is a bare template.
- No stock lifestyle imagery.

## 7. Hero section

### My recommendation, and why it is not a 3D video

You asked for a 3D site with a hero video, products and categories below.

[Likely] elaborate WebGL scenes and cinematic hero loops over generic product shots are the visual signature of stores that hold no stock. That aesthetic became a tell *because* stores with nothing real reach for spectacle to fill the space where proof should be. [Guessing] your Kenyan buyer has been burned by one of those and pattern-matches within two seconds.

**The unanswerable question: a hero video of what?** [Certain] you have no products in hand. So the footage would be stock, AI-generated, or supplier clips — the same source as the imagery that already makes the site read as assembled rather than stocked. It would be a bigger, more expensive version of the existing problem.

**The Kenya-specific cost.** [Likely] a compressed hero video runs 3–10MB; a Three.js scene with models and textures adds 2–5MB. That is **10–15MB of payload** before a visitor has decided they want anything. On mobile, on metered data, some share of visitors will close the tab specifically because your homepage is consuming their bundle. [Certain] you chose WordPress to save KSh 45,000–61,000 a year; spending that saving on payload weight, served from KSh 2,500/year shared hosting, produces a worse store than Shopify would have given you.

**And it contradicts §3–4.** Restraint, legibility, plainness and specificity are not style preferences — they are the mechanism by which an unknown store reads as trustworthy. Cinematic production value says *look how much we spent* to a buyer asking *will you actually ship this.*

### The recommended hero

Static. One image. Loads in under a second.

```
[ Photograph: three gift sets, boxed, on your one surface, in your one light ]

Branded gift sets for Nairobi companies

Designed, packed and delivered in five days.
Minimum order 10 sets. From KSh 2,800 each.

[ See gift sets ]   [ Request a quote ]
```

Below the fold, in order:

1. **Three category tiles** — Gift sets, Personalised, Apparel
2. **Proof row** — delivery areas, payment methods accepted, order minimum, lead time. Four plain facts with icons.
3. **The packing clip** (below) — 15 seconds, muted, click-to-play, not autoplay
4. **Featured sets** — three products maximum, with real photographs
5. **Bulk order call-to-action** — one line and a button to the quote form

### The two motion exceptions

Motion is not banned. **Decorative** motion is. The test: does it answer a question the buyer actually has?

**Exception 1 — the box opening.** On product pages, not the homepage. Closed box; click; lid lifts; contents lay out with labels. This replaces four photographs and a paragraph, so it earns its bytes. [Likely] a few hundred KB if built as an image sequence rather than WebGL. This is the one place where 3D could be justified, and only here. Lazy-load it below the fold.

**Exception 2 — the packing clip.** 15 seconds, phone-shot, unpolished. Your hands, your table, tape, label, handed to a rider. [Likely] the highest-trust video you can make, costs nothing, and does the job a cinematic hero cannot: it proves a human being exists at your end. Under 2MB, muted, click-to-play, never blocking first paint. Reuse it as Instagram content.

**The pattern: motion as evidence, not atmosphere.**

### If you build the 3D hero anyway

Your call, and here is the honest spec rather than a second attempt to talk you out of it.

| Constraint | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Total hero payload | Hard ceiling 2MB. Beyond that, mobile abandonment is not worth it. |
| Video | H.264 + WebM, 1280×720 max, 8–10s loop, silent, `poster` frame set, `preload="none"` |
| Mobile | Serve the poster image only. No video, no WebGL, under 480px. Non-negotiable. |
| 3D | If Three.js: single model, draco-compressed, textures ≤1024px, one light. Static fallback image mandatory. |
| Hosting | Shared hosting will not serve this acceptably. Budget for mid-tier hosting plus Cloudflare's free CDN. |
| Load strategy | Hero text and call-to-action render first, in HTML. Media loads after. The page must be usable before the spectacle arrives. |
| Performance floor | Largest Contentful Paint under 2.5s on 3G. Test on a real phone on Safaricom data, not on your laptop. |
| Content prerequisite | Footage must be **your** products, shot by you. Stock or AI footage makes the problem worse, not better. |

## 9. Limited offers

**Verdict: do not build this as a permanent category now.** [Likely] it will actively hurt you.

### Why

Scarcity converts only when demand is already demonstrated. On a store with no reviews, no sales history and no visible customers, a "limited offers" section does not read as opportunity — it reads as manufactured pressure. It is the same grammar as every fake countdown timer, and [Likely] Kenyan online shoppers are unusually well-calibrated on that tell. You already have four sold-out items broadcasting ambiguous signal; invented urgency on top of that compounds it.

**The worse structural problem: you would be discounting before establishing a full price.** [Certain] nothing has ever sold at KSh 5,500. So "was 5,500, now 4,400" is not a discount, it is a claim you cannot support. You would destroy your price anchor before it exists.

### The version that works

**Real constraints, plainly stated.** Production capacity and a deadline — the way a corporate buyer actually plans.

> **December delivery**
> 12 sets available for delivery before 20 December. Order by 15 November.
> Personalisation adds 5 working days.

That is true scarcity, it is verifiable, and it mirrors your buyer's own planning cycle. Build it as a **seasonal page**, appearing 6–8 weeks before a season and coming down afterwards — not a permanent nav item.

### When to revisit

Add a genuine offers category once you have: 20+ completed orders, visible reviews, and an established full price with sales history behind it. Not before.

## 11. Build specification

### Stack

[Certain] self-hosted WordPress. Not WordPress.com — its commerce tier costs more than Shopify Basic.

| Layer | Choice | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Hosting | Kenyan shared or mid-tier — compare Truehost, HostAfrica, Sasahost, HostPinnacle | [Likely] KSh 2,500–12,000/yr |
| CDN | Cloudflare free tier | Free |
| SSL | Let's Encrypt | Free |
| Theme | Astra, Kadence, Blocksy or GeneratePress | Free |
| Cache | LiteSpeed Cache or WP Super Cache | Free |
| Security | Wordfence free tier | Free |
| Backups | UpdraftPlus — **configure on day one** | Free |
| SEO | Rank Math or Yoast | Free |
| Forms | Fluent Forms or WPForms Lite — for the quote request | Free |

**Hosting caveats.** [Likely] most Kenyan hosting price data available online comes from hosts' own marketing blogs — treat quoted figures as vendor best case. [Certain] one warning that recurs even in that material is that entry-level shared hosting is not recommended for stores handling real transactions, because a busy neighbour on the same server slows you down. Also check renewal pricing: several hosts double the rate in year two.

### Phase 1 — brochure site, no cart

Recommended starting point. WordPress with no WooCommerce at all. Products as plain pages, a quote form, payment details on the delivery page.

Rationale: [Likely] a company ordering 40 gift sets requests a quote and pays by transfer. Zero checkout to maintain, zero transaction fees, zero payment security surface. Buildable in a weekend for roughly KSh 3,000 a year.

### Phase 2 — WooCommerce, when justified

Add a cart when either: monthly revenue exceeds roughly KSh 40,000, or a buyer tells you they could not order because there was no way to pay online.

Payment options at that point, all with free plugins: **IntaSend**, **Pesapal**, **Paystack**, **Flutterwave**. [Certain] there is also a free plugin that connects directly to Safaricom's Daraja API for Lipa na M-Pesa paybill and till numbers, including STK push — fiddlier to set up, but it skips the gateway's cut.

### Gateway fee comparison

[Certain] Shopify Payments is not available in Kenya, so a Shopify store must use a third-party gateway and pay Shopify's surcharge on top.

| | Shopify Basic | WooCommerce |
|---|---|---|
| Gateway fee | 2.5–3% | 2.5–3% |
| Shopify surcharge | **+2%** | 0% |
| **Per sale** | **~4.5–5%** | **~2.5–3%** |
| Subscription | [Likely] KSh 45,000–61,000/yr | KSh 2,500–12,000/yr hosting |

[Certain] Pesapal charges 2.5% flat; IntaSend charges 3% on M-Pesa and 3.8% on cards; both apply on either platform. The 2% is a Shopify surcharge for operating outside its supported payment regions.

### Before you install anything

[Certain] WordPress stores the site URL in the database. Changing domains later means updating configuration and chasing hardcoded links. **Settle the name first** (§2).

### Performance targets

- Homepage total weight under 1MB, including images
- Largest Contentful Paint under 2.5s on 3G
- All images WebP, lazy-loaded below the fold
- Test on a real phone on Safaricom data, not on your laptop over wifi
